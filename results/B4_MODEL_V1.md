## 0. Bar provenance — a discrepancy found and resolved before the gate was scored

The gate-1 bar frozen in `docs/B4_IMPUTATION_PLAN.md` is
`0.66 / 2.09 / 4.37 / 7.55 / 14.91 / 15.16, ALL 11.46`. Re-deriving `B7` from the committed
week-1 code **did not reproduce it** — it gave `0.66 / 2.25 / 4.79 / 8.58 / 16.98 / 15.59,
ALL 12.54`. Re-running `tools/imputation_b4_week1.py --explore` unchanged reproduced *my* number,
not the doc's, so this was not a v1 bug.

Cause, found by sweeping the one free parameter (vote EMA half-life), everything else fixed:

| vote half-life | B7 holdout per bucket | ALL |
|---|---|---|
| **0.04 s** | **0.66 / 2.09 / 4.37 / 7.55 / 14.91 / 15.16** | **11.46** ← the frozen bar |
| 0.25 s | 0.67 / 2.13 / 4.44 / 7.66 / 15.10 / 15.31 | 11.60 |
| 1.0 s | 0.68 / 2.16 / 4.55 / 7.91 / 15.63 / 15.25 | 11.82 |
| 2.0 s | 0.66 / 2.18 / 4.62 / 8.10 / 16.05 / 15.49 | 12.07 |
| 5.0 s | 0.66 / 2.21 / 4.73 / 8.36 / 16.60 / 15.39 | 12.30 |
| 10.0 s (`VOTE_HALFLIFE_S` default) | 0.66 / 2.25 / 4.79 / 8.58 / 16.98 / 15.59 | 12.54 |
| 20.0 s | 0.66 / 2.23 / 4.88 / 8.69 / 17.25 / 15.36 | 12.60 |
| 40.0 s | 0.66 / 2.24 / 4.88 / 8.92 / 17.65 / 15.66 | 12.87 |

The frozen bar was produced with `--vote-halflife 0.04` (one frame — effectively no EMA at all:
the role offset is just the player's last observed displacement from the voted centroid), while
`synthesizer/imputation_features.py` still carries `VOTE_HALFLIFE_S = 10.0` with the comment
"selected on TRAIN (Game 1) only". Corroboration that 0.04 s is the right reading: the doc also
records B7's CALIB ALL as 9.92 m, and 0.04 s reproduces exactly 9.92 m (10.0 s gives 10.98 m).

**Action taken:** v1 is anchored on the bar's own configuration (0.04 s), so anchor and bar are the
same object, as §2.1 requires. `tools/imputation_b4_v1.py` defaults to `BAR_HALFLIFE_S = 0.04` and
prints the discrepancy at the top of every run.

**Two open items for the orchestrator (not fixed here — they touch a committed frozen artifact):**

1. `VOTE_HALFLIFE_S = 10.0` in `synthesizer/imputation_features.py` does not match the value used
   for the frozen bar. Either the constant or its docstring is wrong.
2. Shorter half-life is monotonically better for B7 over three orders of magnitude, and 0.04 s is
   the *edge of the tested grid*. The "EMA role offset" of arXiv 2607.11548 is, at our optimum,
   not an EMA. That is a finding about the published method worth stating in the thesis.

**Disclosed earlier run.** Before finding the above, a complete gate run was executed on the 10.0 s
anchor (run #1). Its gate-1 verdicts were the same shape — LOSE at 0-1s (0.71 vs 0.66), BEAT in the
other five, ALL 8.10 vs 12.54. Nothing was tuned on the holdout in either run; run #2 (this report)
is the one where anchor == bar. Both are reported rather than one being quietly replaced.

## 2. Gate 1 — RMSE per horizon bucket, HOLDOUT, run once

Bar = B7 (training-free, causal). Paired 1-minute block-bootstrap CI on `RMSE(v1) − RMSE(B7)`,
400 replicates. `n_eps` = distinct occlusion episodes = the honest effective sample size.

| horizon | n | n_eps | B7 bar | v1 RMSE | diff [95% block CI] | v1 median | B7 median | offline oracle | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-1s | 51 113 | 2 128 | 0.66 | 0.68 | +0.02 [−0.00, +0.05] | 0.21 | 0.15 | 0.96 | **tie** |
| 1-3s | 82 286 | 1 934 | 2.09 | **1.90** | −0.19 [−0.25, −0.13] | 1.15 | 1.18 | 2.62 | **BEAT** |
| 3-5s | 62 854 | 1 409 | 4.37 | **3.81** | −0.56 [−0.72, −0.41] | 2.61 | 2.86 | 4.57 | **BEAT** |
| 5-10s | 107 320 | 1 122 | 7.55 | **6.07** | −1.48 [−2.01, −0.98] | 4.23 | 5.18 | 7.25 | **BEAT** |
| 10-30s | 190 552 | 657 | 14.91 | **9.74** | −5.17 [−6.80, −3.35] | 6.57 | 9.90 | 10.43 | **BEAT** |
| 30s+ | 150 667 | 182 | 15.16 | **11.24** | −3.92 [−4.60, −3.08] | 5.89 | 8.98 | 12.68 | **BEAT** |
| **ALL** | 644 792 | 2 128 | 11.46 | **8.10** | −3.36 [−4.27, −2.53] | 3.96 | 5.37 | 9.03 | **BEAT** |

**Reading it strictly.** The plan asks for "at or below the bar in *every* bucket, with the win
surviving the CI". Five buckets are significant wins. 0-1s is 2 cm *above* the bar with a CI that
straddles zero — a statistical tie at the velocity-extrapolation noise floor, exactly the regime
the plan said had no headroom ("matching the bar in 0-1s and 1-3s is a pass"). On that
pre-registered reading gate 1 **passes**; on a literal "≤ in every bucket" reading it fails by
0.02 m at 0-1s. Both statements are in this document deliberately.

**Non-monotonicity preserved, not fixed:** 30s+ (11.24) is worse than 10-30s (9.74) for v1, whereas
for the bar 30s+ (15.16) is *better* than 10-30s (14.91). v1 keeps improving where gaps are long
and the ball has moved; the bar's 30s+ advantage came from low-mobility stoppage situations, which
v1 also exploits (30s+ median error 5.89 m).

**v1 beats the offline oracle at long horizons** (10-30s: 9.74 vs 10.43; 30s+: 11.24 vs 12.68;
ALL: 8.10 vs 9.03). This deserves scrutiny, so: linear interpolation over a 20 s gap is a poor use
of the future sighting — it draws a straight line through a path that curved — whereas v1 has the
current visible team structure and the ball. The oracle is still better at 3-5s and comparable at
5-10s. We are not claiming v1 is "better than knowing the future"; we are saying straight-line
interpolation is a weak way to use it at long gaps.

## 4. Abstention

### Layer A — skill horizon (`b*`), derived on CALIB

| horizon | v1 (CALIB) | B7 (CALIB) | diff [95% CI] | significantly better? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-1s | 0.42 | 0.41 | [−0.01, +0.02] | **no** |
| 1-3s | 1.82 | 2.08 | [−0.32, −0.21] | yes |
| 3-5s | 3.70 | 4.36 | [−0.78, −0.54] | yes |
| 5-10s | 6.11 | 7.40 | [−1.61, −0.96] | yes |
| 10-30s | 9.52 | 13.73 | [−5.44, −2.93] | yes |
| 30s+ | 8.29 | 12.53 | [−5.09, −3.10] | yes |

`b* = 0-1s`.

**A protocol defect, disclosed on CALIB before the holdout was opened.** The rule "abstain at and
beyond `b*`" imports the weather-forecasting assumption that skill decays monotonically with
horizon. Ours does the opposite: the only bucket without a significant win is the *shortest* one,
so the literal rule abstains on **100% of samples** — a model that beats the bar by 5.2 m at 10-30s
is silenced by a 1 cm tie at 0-1s. We applied the rule literally anyway (pre-registration wins over
post-hoc convenience) and report the outcome, alongside a clearly-labelled variant:

| horizon | asserted, pre-registered rule | asserted, per-bucket variant (NOT pre-registered) |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1s | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| 1-3s | 0.0% | 100.0% |
| 3-5s | 0.0% | 100.0% |
| 5-10s | 0.0% | 100.0% |
| 10-30s | 0.0% | 100.0% |
| 30s+ | 0.0% | 99.7% |
| **overall** | **0.0%** | **92.0%** |

Variant asserted-subset RMSE 8.38 m vs B7 11.89 m on the same subset. **Recommendation for the
supervisor:** re-state layer A as "abstain in any bucket where v1 does not significantly beat the
bar" rather than "at and beyond the first such bucket", and re-freeze. That is a change to the
rule, so it needs sign-off, not a code edit.

On abstain the emitted coordinate is the **last-seen position with `asserted=False`**
(`imputation_v1.emit`), never a silent hold-last fallback.

### Layer B — SGR reject option

Binary-searched on CALIB only, δ=0.05, risk target = B7's CALIB RMSE (9.92 m), 95% one-sided
block-bootstrap upper bound on selective RMSE.

**`R_max` = 30.4339 m**, frozen and printed before the holdout run. It accepts **100%** of CALIB
(selective RMSE 6.99 m, 95% upper bound 7.49 m — comfortably under 9.92 m). Layer B therefore
rejects nothing: the model already meets the risk guarantee at full coverage, so the reject option
is inactive by construction rather than by tuning. Reported as such rather than made to look useful
by lowering the target.

Wording note: plan §3.3 says "the *smallest* `R_max` such that selective RMSE ≤ bar"; taken
literally that is `R_max = 0` (abstain everywhere), which is vacuous. We used the published SGR
search — the *largest* threshold whose 95% risk bound still meets the bar, i.e. maximal coverage.

AUGRC (generalized risk: loss summed over accepted ÷ *total* N) is reported in preference to bare
AURC per arXiv 2407.01032, because AURC hides how errors distribute across the accepted set.

Curves are monotone in the right direction in every bucket except 0-1s, where the 10% most
confident subset (0.24 m) is *worse* than the 25% subset (0.19 m) — at that scale `r90` sits on the
0.25 m width floor and carries almost no ranking information. The steepest, most useful curve is
30s+: the most confident half of long-gap samples is at 5.19 m against 11.24 m for the full set, so
the confidence score genuinely separates stoppage-type gaps from live-play gaps.

## 6. Threats to validity (all disclosed, none fixed post-hoc)

1. **Learnt censoring geometry.** Being off-camera is itself information — the simulator censors a
   hard rectangle around the ball, and `ball_x` is a feature. Fraction of holdout positions falling
   inside the visible x-band: truth 11.5%, last-seen 32.9%, B7 anchor 18.7%, **v1 14.9%**. v1 sits
   closer to the truth than the anchor does, i.e. it has partly learnt "the player cannot be where
   the camera is looking". That is causally legitimate (a real pipeline also knows who is
   undetected), but the *hard rectangle* is a simulator artefact, so **this is the single biggest
   transfer risk to real broadcast footage** and must be re-measured in M3. (Caveat: the diagnostic
   tests the x-half-width only; the y constraint also censors, which is why 11.5% of hidden truths
   legitimately sit inside the x-band.)
2. **Half-split holdout.** CALIB and HOLDOUT are two halves of the same match — same teams, same
   players, same tactics. Weaker than an unseen match, as the plan already concedes. Metrica Game 3
   passing its format audit remains the highest-value data win available.
3. **Anchor `v0` observability.** The frozen anchor's last-seen velocity is computed on the full
   truth over a 0.5 s window, so 3.1% of samples have a `v0` partly spanning off-camera frames
   (mean |diff| 0.045 m/s; recomputing B5 with observable-only `v0` moved it 13.24 → 13.22 m). v1's
   *own* `v0_x/v0_y` features use the visibility-masked field. Inherited from week 1, unchanged.
4. **Effective sample size.** 30s+ has 150 667 rows but only **182 occlusion episodes**; 10-30s has
   657. Per-bucket CIs use 1-minute blocks precisely because of this, and the CI widths at long
   horizons (±1.5 m) reflect it.
5. **Hyper-parameters and `R_max` come from CALIB**, so CALIB numbers are not reportable results —
   they appear here only as the frozen-parameter trail.
6. **Two gate runs exist** (§0). Run #1 used the 10.0 s anchor; run #2 (this one) uses the bar's own
   0.04 s anchor. Neither was tuned on the holdout; both are reported.

## 7. Honest verdict

**Did v1 earn its place over the training-free anchor? Yes — decisively, and not by construction.**

- Gate 1: **5/6 buckets beaten with non-overlapping CIs, 1/6 tied**, ALL 8.10 m vs 11.46 m
  (−29%). The wins are largest exactly where the plan predicted the money was: 10-30s
  (14.91 → 9.74, −35%) and 30s+ (15.16 → 11.24, −26%). Residual-on-anchor did its job — the learner
  could only tie or win, and it won.
- Gate 2: **6/6 at both 50% and 90%**, no ACI needed. Better calibrated than the zero-learning
  fallback, with per-sample rather than per-bucket widths.
- Gate 4 (abstention): the machinery works and is frozen, but **both layers are degenerate on this
  data** — layer A abstains everywhere because of a monotonicity assumption our error curve
  violates, and layer B abstains nowhere because the model already clears the risk target at full
  coverage. Neither degeneracy was chosen after seeing the holdout; both are reported as protocol
  findings, and layer A needs a supervisor-approved re-statement before it is useful.
- The novelty claim ("causal, calibrated, abstaining, from a broadcast pipeline") survives on the
  first two legs and is honest-but-unexercised on the third.

**What I would not claim from this run.** That v1 is "better than the offline oracle" as a general
statement — it beats straight-line interpolation at long gaps, which is a statement about that
baseline's weakness. That the 10-30s improvement will transfer to real footage — a measurable part
of it may be learnt censoring geometry (§6.1). That abstention has been validated — it has been
implemented and frozen, not exercised.

**Biggest single risk to the whole result:** if the M3 transfer shows the long-horizon gain is
mostly the simulator's rectangular camera window, the headline −35% at 10-30s shrinks. That test is
runnable now and should be scheduled ahead of the §2.3 GRU stretch (which the plan gates behind a
clean v1 holdout — a condition this run has met).
