The official record for this fixture shows exactly one goal, second half, to Manchester United.

The goal happens 24 seconds after our last measurable passage

We do not show you the goal. The moment the ball goes in, the broadcast cuts to the celebration, and every one of those cuts destroys the pitch geometry our measurement depends on. Our gate refuses the passage, and the clip jumps straight to a card instead.

We tested this on the whole corpus before accepting it as fixed, not just for this match. We took every goal peak our detector produces -- 30 peaks across 10 matches -- and scored the best window of 120, 90, 60 and 30 seconds ending at each one against our quality gate. 112 windows scored. Zero cleared the gate. All 112 failed on the same clause: pitch geometry, never players or ball. The best two-minute window at any goal in the whole corpus reaches 50.9% frame coverage against a required 85%.

The reason is not a weakness in our tracker. It is what a broadcast is. A goal is immediately followed by a replay, a close-up of the scorer, the bench, the crowd -- and every one of those cuts breaks the pitch geometry the measurement rests on. Goals are the least measurable moments in a football broadcast. That is an inconvenient thing to learn while building a demo, and a useful thing to know if you are buying one.

What we refuse to say, and why

No per-player statistics

We will not tell you how many passes Bruno Fernandes made, or rank any player's involvement in this match. Across our identity-tracked matches, only 1.0-4.2% of United's attributed passes carry a player's name -- two to five named passes a match. On 90 hand-labelled moments, the precision of naming the player on the ball is 0.35 to 0.40. A rebuilt, more constrained version of the naming logic did not beat that number. Any per-player total we could show you would be built on evidence that is right barely more often than a coin flip.

No calibrated uncertainty regions

Our tracking clips draw a shaded region around players we cannot currently see, showing where a model predicts they probably stand. Those regions were designed to be calibrated: the shaded 50% region should contain the true player 50% of the time, the 90% region 90% of the time. On real broadcast footage they do not. Measured against 4,900 real player re-appearances across four matches, the regions cover the true position 21.2% of the time when they claim 50%, and 55.4% of the time when they claim 90%. Every one of 14 testable buckets fails. We publish the measured numbers on every frame instead of the claimed ones.

Both refusals above are measurements, not caution for its own sake. We built each capability, tested it against real evidence, and it did not clear the bar we set for it. That is the honest output for this match, exactly as it would be for any match in this corpus.
